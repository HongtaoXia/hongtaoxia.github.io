Research transition

Clinically meaningful questions.

I am a Master's candidate in Mechanical Engineering at Henan University of Technology, ranked first in my cohort. My graduate research combines computer vision, dataset curation, controlled experimentation, embedded control, and edge deployment.

My current research has trained me to solve a problem central to morphology-based analysis: finding and evaluating small, irregular, and partially occluded structures under variable imaging conditions. I have built datasets, developed detection and segmentation workflows, and tested models through ablation, cross-architecture, multi-seed, and controlled experiments.

For doctoral training, I want to extend this foundation to computational hematology: linking blood-cell morphology with malignancy and aging, and developing rigorous clinical-data analyses for external or synthetic control arms.

Closest technical match

Blood-cell morphology in blood cancers

My strongest fit is image-based morphology. Experience with small-object localization, semantic segmentation, boundary-aware learning, annotation quality control, and occlusion analysis is transferable to cell identification and morphology-aware phenotyping.

Quantitative extension

Aging and morphological change

Controlled comparisons, regression and correlation analysis, repeated runs, and multi-seed validation provide a basis for studying morphology across age groups while distinguishing biological variation from model and imaging variability.

Primary learning direction

Synthetic control arms for clinical trials

I want to build formal expertise in cohort construction, real-world data harmonization, causal inference, outcome modeling, and sensitivity analysis for external or synthetic control arms.
